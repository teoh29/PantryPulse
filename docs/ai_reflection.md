# AI Integration Reflection — Project_23051105

### What AI Was Most Useful For
The adoption of AI in developing my pantry management system gave me a significant boost in productivity. However, it also needs to be constantly monitored for architectural integrity. In particular, AI tools proved most helpful in speeding up repetitive UI layout code and producing clean ScalaFX CSS styling rules. Rather than wasting hours reading documentation and figuring out drop shadows & padding syntax, I used AI to produce the base CSS.  As noted in Template-2 entry #4, I prompted Gemini for modern card views and navigation sidebars. While it provided excellent raw property strings, it could not see the bigger picture of my application's theme. I used its raw output, renamed its style variables, stripped out common tokens to my UiTheme object, and tweaked the padding constraints to my desired layout. This allowed me to focus on the business logic, instead of on pixel pushing.
### Where AI Misled Me
However, when it was heavily used, it was prone to hallucinate library-specific idioms, such as confusing ScalaFX with JavaFX. A prime example of this occurred in Template-2 entry #7. Also, when I asked how to add button event handlers in the TableView form, Gemini responded with the standard Java method right away: “setOnAction(new EventHandler[ActionEvent] {...}). If I had blindly pasted this, it would have ruined the idiomatic Scala codebase I was building. I had to reject its suggestion and write the handler from scratch with the proper ScalaFX closure syntax onAction = handle { ... }. The LLMs were often confused between the Java paradigms with pure Scala conventions, where I had to constantly review their output against functional programming requirements.
### What I Did That AI Could Not Do
Overall, the AI functioned as a good assistant and syntax reference, but it lacks of domain context and systemic architectural judgement. AI could not reason about the actual real-world constraints of the SDG-1 domain. For example, in Template-2 entry #6, Claude developed a simple sorting algorithm for inventory allocation but did not address the actual conditions in the pantry, such as avoiding single-family inventory hoarding. I needed to intervene and develop the mathematical capping logic of the household size. Moreover, I was solely responsible for implementing strict rules for the entire system, like zero mutable variables in the domain layer, and manually using monadic Either and Try for risky operations.  Moreover, the AI often produced classes that were very similar and repetitive. I needed to work out the common logic and extract it into common traits and abstract classes to maintain a strict adherence to the OOP standards. The AI gave me fragmented answers, but stitching them together into a strong, rubric-matching Scala application in which I can stand confidently in my viva was entirely my own work.
